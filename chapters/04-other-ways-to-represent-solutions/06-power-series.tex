\subsection{Noncharacteristic surfaces and determination of the full jet}
For a quasilinear equation of order $k$, write
\[
 \sum_{|\gamma|=k}a_\gamma(D^{k-1}u,\ldots,u,x)D^\gamma u
                     +a_0(D^{k-1}u,\ldots,u,x)=0 .
\]
Its principal polynomial is
$P(x,\zeta)=\sum_{|\gamma|=k}a_\gamma\zeta^\gamma$,
with coefficients evaluated on the chosen lower-order jet.
Cauchy data on a hypersurface $\Gamma$ prescribe
$u,\partial_\nu u,\ldots,\partial_\nu^{k-1}u$.
Here $\partial_\nu^j$ is the contraction of $D^ju$ with $\nu^{\otimes j}$
at the point, as in the source; iterating a variable normal field
would also create lower-order curvature terms.

\begin{theorem}[Source Theorem 1]
If $P(x,\nu)\ne0$ along the specified data, then the PDE and the
Cauchy data uniquely determine every derivative of any smooth solution
along $\Gamma$.
This is a determination statement, not an existence theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{expandedproof}
On $\Gamma=\{x_n=0\}$ the derivatives of the Cauchy functions in
$x'=(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1})$ determine all derivatives of total order at
most $k$, except $\partial_n^ku$.
Its coefficient in the PDE is $a_{ke_n}=P(x,e_n)\ne0$.
Solving the PDE gives this missing derivative.
Its tangential derivatives then give every derivative with exactly
$k$ normal differentiations.
Differentiate the solved PDE once in $x_n$ to find
$\partial_n^{k+1}u$; all right-side normal orders are now at most $k$.
Repeat, inducting on normal order and allowing arbitrary tangential
differentiation at each stage.

For a curved surface use coordinates $y=\Psi(x)$ with
$\Gamma=\{y_n=0\}$. The chain rule shows that the coefficient of
$\partial_{y_n}^kv$ in $u=v\circ\Psi$ is
\[
 \sum_{|\gamma|=k}a_\gamma(D\Psi_n)^\gamma .
\]
On $\Gamma$, $D\Psi_n$ is a nonzero multiple of $\nu$, so homogeneity
and the noncharacteristic condition make this coefficient nonzero.
The prescribed normal derivatives convert to $y_n$ derivatives by
a triangular system: at each order its leading coefficient is a
nonzero power of $\partial_\nu\Psi_n$, while lower-order terms and
tangential derivatives are already known.
Apply the flat argument and transform the resulting jets back.
\end{expandedproof}
\Needspace{45mm}
\PDEAnnotation{知道所有导数不等于知道函数}{光滑函数可在一点具有全零 Taylor 系数而在邻域不为零，例如 $e^{-1/x^2}$ 的零延拓。要由 jet 恢复解，需要额外的解析性或其他唯一性理论。}
For a surface $\{\rho=0\}$, use $P(x,D\rho)\ne0$.
For the wave equation the principal polynomial is
$\tau^2-|\xi|^2$, giving characteristic light cones.
For the heat equation, considered as a total-order-two PDE,
the principal polynomial is $-|\xi|^2$:
the time-zero surface has $(\xi,\tau)=(0,1)$ and is characteristic.
This convention does not negate forward parabolic well-posedness.

\subsection{Analytic functions and coefficientwise majorants}
An analytic function near zero is represented there by a convergent
power series $f(x)=\sum_\gamma f_\gamma x^\gamma$,
$f_\gamma=D^\gamma f(0)/\gamma!$.
A formal series $F=\sum F_\gamma x^\gamma$ majorizes $f$ if
$F_\gamma\geq|f_\gamma|$ for every $\gamma$; write $F\gg f$.
\begin{lemma}[Source majorant lemma, with the operations made explicit]
If $F$ has nonnegative coefficients and converges near zero, then
$F\gg f$ implies absolute convergence of $f$ in a smaller neighborhood.
Every analytic series has a majorant
$C/(1-(x_1+\cdots+x_d)/r)$ after shrinking $r$.
Majorization is preserved by addition, multiplication,
partial differentiation, and composition with zero-constant-term series.
\end{lemma}
\begin{expandedproof}
Coefficientwise comparison gives
$\sum|f_\gamma x^\gamma|\leq F(|x_1|,\ldots,|x_d|)$.
Analytic convergence on a smaller polydisc implies
$|f_\gamma|\leq C r^{-|\gamma|}$.
But the coefficient of $x^\gamma$ in the displayed rational series is
$Cr^{-|\gamma|}|\gamma|!/\gamma!\geq Cr^{-|\gamma|}$.
The multinomial expansion therefore proves the comparison.
For products apply the triangle inequality to the finite coefficient
convolution; for derivatives multiply each coefficient by its
nonnegative integer exponent. Composition is a sum of such products.
The zero constant term ensures that each fixed total-degree
coefficient receives only finitely many composition contributions.
These facts apply componentwise to vector and matrix series.
\end{expandedproof}
\Needspace{45mm}
\PDEAnnotation{控制函数值不等于控制系数}{majorant 比较的是每个 Taylor 系数的绝对值。它之所以能控制递推解，是因为求导、乘积和复合只通过非负组合数来组合这些系数。}

\subsection{Cauchy--Kovalevskaya: recurrence and actual convergence}
\label{sec:4-ck}
After flattening an analytic noncharacteristic surface and subtracting
analytic extensions of the Cauchy data, a quasilinear equation reduces
to a first-order system for the derivatives of order below $k$.
Write the normal coordinate as $t$ and the $d=n-1$ tangential
coordinates as $x'$:
\[
 U_t=\sum_{j=1}^dB_j(U,x')U_{x_j}+c(U,x'),\qquad U(x',0)=0 .
 \tag{CK4}
\]
Here $U$ has $m$ components.
If coefficients originally depend on $t$, add a component
$T_t=1$, $T(x',0)=0$, and replace explicit $t$ by $T$;
this reduces to the displayed time-independent coefficient form.
\begin{theorem}[Source Theorem 2]
For analytic $B_j,c$ near $(0,0)$, (CK4) has a unique analytic
solution in a neighborhood of zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{expandedproof}[the explicit majorant, fully verified]
\paragraph{1. Recursive formal solution.}
Write $U=\sum_{\ell\geq1}t^\ell U_\ell(x')$.
Equating the coefficient of $t^\ell$ gives
\[
 (\ell+1)U_{\ell+1}
 =[t^\ell]\left\{\sum_jB_j(U,x')U_{x_j}+c(U,x')\right\}.
\]
The right side only uses $U_1,\ldots,U_\ell$ (and their tangential
derivatives); $U_1=c(0,x')$.
Equivalently, each joint Taylor coefficient is determined recursively
by smaller normal order.
Composition is legitimate as a formal joint series since $U(0,0)=0$.
No negative combinatorial coefficients occur in the operations on
the right. This proves formal uniqueness, but not yet convergence.

\paragraph{2. Dominate the coefficient functions.}
Choose $r,C>0$ so that every entry of every $B_j$ and $c$ is
majorized by
\[
 F(Z,x')=\frac{Cr}{r-\sum_{j=1}^dx_j-\sum_{\ell=1}^mZ_\ell}.
\]
The preceding lemma supplies such a common choice for the finite
collection of analytic entries.
The comparison system has all its components equal to a scalar $v$.
With $s=\sum_jx_j$, its equation is
\[
 v_t=\frac{Cr}{r-s-mv}(md\,v_s+1),\qquad v(s,0)=0.
 \tag{MAJ4}
\]
Each of the $d$ matrix rows sums $m$ identical derivatives, explaining
the factor $md$.

\paragraph{3. Solve and check the majorant.}
Let $N=m(d+1)$ and set
\[
 v(s,t)=\frac{r-s-\sqrt{(r-s)^2-2NCr\,t}}N .
\]
Use the square-root branch equal to $r-s$ near $(0,0)$.
It is analytic near zero, is zero at $t=0$, and obeys
\[
 (r-s)v-\frac N2v^2=Cr\,t .
\]
Implicit differentiation gives
$v_t=Cr/(r-s-Nv)$ and $v_s=v/(r-s-Nv)$.
Thus
\[
 \frac{Cr}{r-s-mv}(md\,v_s+1)
 =\frac{Cr}{r-s-mv}
   \frac{r-s-Nv+md\,v}{r-s-Nv}=v_t,
\]
because $N=md+m$.
The expansion of $1-\sqrt{1-z}$ has nonnegative coefficients;
expanding $(r-s)^{-1}$ also does. Hence $v$ is a nonnegative
coefficient majorant in $(x',t)$.

\paragraph{4. Compare recursively and obtain a PDE solution.}
At normal order zero both series vanish.
Assume all coefficients up to normal order $\ell$ of $U$ are
dominated by those of $(v,\ldots,v)$.
The positive-operation rules and coefficient majorization of $B_j,c$
give the same domination for the right side of the recurrence.
Division by $\ell+1>0$ preserves it.
Thus the domination holds at every normal order.
The convergent analytic series $v$ bounds the absolute joint series
of every component of $U$ on a smaller polydisc.
On still smaller polydiscs derivatives also converge normally.
The analytic compositions are therefore genuine functions;
their Taylor coefficients satisfy (CK4), so the analytic identity
holds near zero. Analytic uniqueness follows from the coefficient
recurrence and equality with the Taylor series.
\end{expandedproof}
\Needspace{45mm}
\PDEAnnotation{优级数的作用是证明收敛}{形式递推只确定候选解的系数。显式平方根解控制这些系数的绝对值，才把形式级数变成可求导的解析函数；数据解析本身还没有完成收敛证明。}

\paragraph{Returning to the scalar higher-order equation.}
Include every derivative $D^\gamma u$, $|\gamma|<k$, as a component.
Normal derivatives of lower-order components are other components;
a derivative of a top-order component either is a tangential derivative
of another top-order component or, for the pure normal term, is obtained
from the solved original PDE.
This yields the quasilinear system above.
The formal scalar jet determined by the noncharacteristic recursion
satisfies these derivative identities and hence the formal system.
Its formally unique system solution is exactly the analytic series
constructed above. Consequently all compatibility identities between
the components hold as analytic identities.
The zeroth component really is a scalar solution with the required
derivatives; introducing a vector of independent unknowns did not
silently discard compatibility.

For nonzero analytic initial data subtract a polynomial in $t$
with analytic $x'$ coefficients matching the prescribed normal jets,
then incorporate its known terms into the coefficients.
Analytic changes of coordinates preserve analyticity.
This proves the quasilinear analytic noncharacteristic Cauchy theorem.
A fully nonlinear version additionally solves its highest normal
derivative by the analytic implicit function theorem and needs the
corresponding nonzero derivative condition; that broader theorem is
a further result, not proved by the quasilinear reduction alone.
