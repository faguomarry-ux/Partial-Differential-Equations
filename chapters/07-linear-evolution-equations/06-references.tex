\section{References and Proof Dependencies}
\label{sec:7-6}
The primary mathematical source is Lawrence C. Evans, \emph{Partial
Differential Equations}, second edition, Chapter 7, printed pp.~349--423
(local English PDF pp.~366--440). The Chinese scan corresponds to printed
pp.~248--299 (PDF pp.~266--317). Selected comparisons and the source theorem
map are recorded in \texttt{docs/CHAPTER-07.md}.
All English exposition, proof reconstructions, solutions, annotations and
diagrams in this chapter are editorial writing.

Evans points to Ladyzhenskaya, J.-L. Lions, Lions--Magenes and Wloka for
evolution equations; Davies for Harnack's calculation; Krylov,
Ladyzhenskaya--Solonnikov--Uraltseva and Lieberman for further regularity;
John and Treves for the Fourier approach; Friedman and Yosida for semigroups.
Problem 9 is linked to Brezis--Evans, \emph{Archive for Rational Mechanics
and Analysis} 71 (1979), 1--13, and Ladyzhenskaya--Uraltseva.
These source bibliography pointers were not independently consulted.

\paragraph{What is proved and what is assumed.}
The Galerkin constructions, initial-trace passage, integrated-test wave
uniqueness, regularity inductions, smooth principal-part Harnack inequality,
finite propagation under a smooth optical-distance hypothesis, vanishing
viscosity construction, Fourier multiplier bound, generator properties,
contraction Hille--Yosida theorem, and both PDE applications have expanded
proofs. All 16 source exercises have editorial solutions; Problem 9 retains
and estimates the boundary term explicitly.
Hilbert weak compactness, Banach contraction, the closed graph theorem,
Bochner integration, Fourier Plancherel and finite-dimensional Schur
triangularization are prerequisites. Sobolev approximation, traces,
extension and embedding come from Chapter 5; the Dirichlet spectral basis,
Lax--Milgram and elliptic boundary estimates come from Chapter 6.
The local flow-box construction uses classical smooth ODE theory.

\paragraph{Scope boundaries.}
General rough-coefficient Harnack theory, global smooth optical distances,
nonsymmetric $L^2$ well-posedness under real spectrum alone, and analytic
semigroup smoothing are not proved. Our strong maximum proof additionally
assumes time-independent principal coefficients. The Fourier uniqueness
class is specified explicitly; autonomous semigroups require time-independent
coefficients. This chapter has not received independent mathematical review.
