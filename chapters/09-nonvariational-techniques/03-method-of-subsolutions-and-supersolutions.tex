\subsection{Weak order and boundary traces}
Let $f:\R\to\R$ be globally Lipschitz, with constant $L$. For
$\underline u,\overline u\in H^1(U)$, call them a subsolution and a
supersolution of $-\Delta u=f(u)$ when, for all nonnegative $v\in H^1_0(U)$,
\[
 \int D\underline u\cdot Dv\leq\int f(\underline u)v,\qquad
 \int D\overline u\cdot Dv\geq\int f(\overline u)v.
\]
The boundary conditions required below are inequalities of Sobolev traces,
not pointwise values of arbitrary representatives.
\Needspace{50mm}
\begin{theorem}[Ordered existence; source Theorem 1]
If $\underline u\leq\overline u$ a.e. and
$\underline u|_{\partial U}\leq0\leq\overline u|_{\partial U}$,
there is a weak solution $u\in H^1_0(U)$ between them.
The increasing and decreasing iterations give respectively the least and
greatest weak solution in this order interval.
\end{theorem}
\begin{expandedproof}[comparison, domination, and strong convergence]
Choose $\lambda\geq L$, $\lambda>0$, so $h(s)=f(s)+\lambda s$ is
nondecreasing. Starting from $u_0=\underline u$, solve
\begin{equation}\label{eq:9-order-iteration}
 (-\Delta+\lambda)u_{k+1}=h(u_k),\qquad u_{k+1}\in H^1_0(U).
\end{equation}
The forcing is in $L^2$ and Lax--Milgram gives every iterate.
Subtract the equation for $u_1$ from the subsolution inequality after
adding the same $\lambda\underline u$ term. Test by
$v=(\underline u-u_1)_+$. The trace inequality makes $v\in H^1_0$,
and the Sobolev truncation rule gives
$\|Dv\|_2^2+\lambda\|v\|_2^2\leq0$. Hence $u_0\leq u_1$.

If $u_{k-1}\leq u_k$, subtract successive equations and test by
$(u_k-u_{k+1})_+$. Since $h(u_{k-1})\leq h(u_k)$, the same reasoning
gives $u_k\leq u_{k+1}$. Comparing with the supersolution using
$(u_{k+1}-\overline u)_+$ gives $u_{k+1}\leq\overline u$ whenever
$u_k\leq\overline u$. Thus
\[
 \underline u=u_0\leq u_1\leq\cdots\leq u_k\leq\overline u.
\]
The pointwise limit $u$ exists a.e. Also
$|u_k|\leq|\underline u|+|\overline u|\in L^2$; dominated convergence
gives $u_k\to u$ in $L^2$. The Lipschitz property gives
$h(u_k)\to h(u)$ in $L^2$. The bounded inverse
$(-\Delta+\lambda)^{-1}:L^2\to H^1_0$ now shows that
$u_{k+1}\to(-\Delta+\lambda)^{-1}h(u)$ strongly in $H^1_0$.
Its $L^2$ limit was $u$, hence $u$ is this inverse image and solves
the nonlinear equation after cancellation of $\lambda u$.

If $v$ is any solution in the interval, induction by the same comparison
gives $u_k\leq v$, hence $u\leq v$. Starting instead at $\overline u$
gives a decreasing sequence and the greatest solution. These extremal
properties are editorial consequences; uniqueness is not asserted.
\end{expandedproof}

\subsection{Local nonlinearities and the limits of comparison}
If the barriers are bounded and $f$ is only Lipschitz on their common
value interval $[a,b]$, extend $f|_{[a,b]}$ to a globally Lipschitz
function (for example by constant continuation outside). The iteration
stays between the barriers, so the resulting equation uses the original
$f$. This is a useful editorial localization of the source's hypothesis.
\Needspace{42mm}
\PDEAnnotation{上下解不保证唯一}{对 $-\Delta u=\lambda_1u$，零解和任意倍数的第一特征函数都是解。顺序构造给出区间内极小、极大解；唯一性需要额外结构。}
If $f$ is nonincreasing, two solutions with the same trace are identical:
testing their difference gives
$\|D(u-v)\|_2^2=\int(f(u)-f(v))(u-v)\leq0$.
More generally uniqueness follows when $\operatorname{Lip}(f)C_P^2<1$.
This uses a quantitative bound; mere smoothness is insufficient.
